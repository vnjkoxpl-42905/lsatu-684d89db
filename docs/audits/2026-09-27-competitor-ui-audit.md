# LSAT platform UI audit: running log

Started 2026-09-27. This is the working comparison for LSAT U, LSAT Lab, 7Sage, AdeptLR, and LSAT Demon. The competitor findings below come from direct interaction with Joshua's signed-in browser on 2026-09-27. LSAT U comparisons are from its current repository unless marked live. A visible control is evidence of an interface, not proof that its data or outcome is reliable. No student records or competitor question text are reproduced here. The LSAT Demon pass excludes Admissions, Crushing 1L, and Tutor at Joshua's request.

## Decisions already made for LSAT U

Joshua accepted these navigation directions on 2026-09-27:

1. **My Work** is one student destination with To do, Completed, and Notes & recaps inside it. Assignment and recap records keep their separate meanings.
2. **Review** holds Wrong answers and Flagged. **Progress** holds Analytics and History. These are separate pages or top-level sections, each with internal tabs and deep links.
3. The teacher has a short, distinct workspace rail plus a clear **View student experience** shortcut. Bootcamps and Activities remain distinct internal teacher Library tabs rather than additional teacher rail items.

Joshua is not producing videos for this phase. Any idea that requires new video production is deferred. Existing videos can stay accessible.

## How to read the log

- **Adopt:** useful fit for Joshua's one-to-one teaching model. **Adapt:** sound behavior, but LSAT Lab's structure or visual treatment needs changing. **Keep:** LSAT U already has meaningful parity. **Defer:** weak fit or high cost now.
- **P0:** prerequisite to trustworthy workflows. **P1:** next product improvement. **P2:** worthwhile after core work. **Later:** requires new content, scale, or a separate product bet.
- **Verified:** seen in the browser or source. **Proposed:** design judgment that still needs a prototype or student test.

## Small interface details worth considering

1. **One primary action per card.** LSAT Lab often presents both Drill and Review on a priority. In LSAT U, place the recommended action first and the alternative as a text action, with the attempt count beside the claim.
2. **Show the size of the next step.** Estimated activity time, question count, and available pool help a student commit. Mark estimates clearly and hide them if unreliable.
3. **Use tabs within a named destination.** Tests, Practice Sets, subject overviews, and video library use tabs effectively. LSAT U's planned My Work, Review, and Progress should retain direct URLs for each tab so reminders and teacher links open the right state.
4. **Offer compact and expanded views.** A concise list should be the default for work and review; an optional table can expose PT/S/Q, result, date, time, source, and notes for power users and teachers.
5. **Use progressive disclosure for filters.** Practice Sets' visible essentials plus Customize are a better model than putting every type and feature into the side rail.
6. **Keep navigation labels visible.** LSAT Lab's compact rail recovers space but costs recognition. A short labeled LSAT U rail can fit fewer, stronger destinations and use icons as support.
7. **Place topic navigation near learning.** LSAT Lab's LR expansion lists 15 question types in the global rail. LSAT U should put such long taxonomies inside the topic or Library page with search, recent topics, and grouped families.
8. **Keep sidebar expansion from covering work.** LSAT Lab's expanded rail shifted or obscured the booking content in the observed viewport. Test LSAT U's flyouts at laptop widths, keyboard focus, and zoom.
9. **Make progress states explicit.** In progress, completed, blind review, hidden score, and flagged should have words or accessible labels, not color and tiny icons alone.
10. **Preserve source context.** A question found from Analytics or History should return to the originating filtered view. LSAT Lab's question finder includes a return URL; LSAT U should maintain the same continuity.
11. **Avoid false precision.** Frequency, average time, difficulty, and adaptive rating need definitions, sample sizes, and outlier handling. LSAT Lab showed several extreme times and zero-question adaptive sessions with long durations in this account.
12. **Avoid decorative emptiness.** Some LSAT Lab pages use a large banner or chart before the first useful action. LSAT U should put the next task or diagnostic evidence within the first screen.

## Visual and interaction details from 7Sage

1. **A short global vocabulary wins.** The Study menu groups tools by the student's intent, while each destination uses local tabs or a table of contents. LSAT U should keep its approved few rail destinations and put Bootcamps, Activities, topic lists, and review subtypes inside their parent pages.
2. **The best screens support two reading speeds.** A test result starts with score and section summary, then offers a dense question table. A study plan starts with the day and lets the student collapse completed work. Use a compact default plus Details for power users and Joshua.
3. **Persistent context reduces backtracking.** In the tester, the passage stays put as explanation, analytics, and notes appear beside it. A student's answer and reasoning should likewise remain visible when Joshua's explanation is opened.
4. **Resume belongs near Start.** Both platforms show in-process work at the launch point. LSAT U should make Continue the first action for unfinished drills and tests, rather than sending students to a separate history page.
5. **A one-click next step is only useful when its reason is visible.** 7Sage's priority row shows frequency, accuracy, goal, Review count, and Drill. LSAT U can provide a teacher-approved priority with attempt count and a plain-language reason before its actions.
6. **Small controls carry teaching intent.** Blind Review choices, Anticipation mode, answer-reveal control, bookmarks, and first-versus-blind answer state reinforce how to practice. Their labels and defaults matter more than visual polish.
7. **Question-level notes are a bridge between pages.** A journal entry should open from My Work when assigned, Review when revisiting the question, and Progress when explaining a pattern. Its source attempt and teacher feedback should remain attached.
8. **Time and source labels prevent misleading interpretation.** A 7Sage result distinguishes LawHub import, includes analytics state, and shows actual against target time. LSAT U should show whether a number came from an assigned drill, independent test, Activity, or bootcamp attempt.
9. **The visual style is calm but often too tall.** Off-white backgrounds, navy serif headings, teal actions, yellow active underlines, broad margins, and thin card borders make 7Sage readable. Large blank areas and long vertically stacked pages hide useful actions below the first screen. Borrow spacing hierarchy, not its exact colors or type.
10. **Settings explain consequences.** 7Sage labels what changes analytic inclusion or review prompts. LSAT U should explain any setting that affects scoring, recommendations, or attempt history in the control's own context.

### AdeptLR design findings

1. **Its restraint is valuable.** Three global destinations are easy to remember, and the question canvas keeps the stimulus and choices easy to read. LSAT U needs more than three because assigned work, teacher feedback, and scheduling are core, but the rail should still stay short.
2. **The drill builder is functionally strong and visually flat.** The live pool count, answer-reveal switch, freshness choice, and LR/RC-specific units are useful micro-interactions. Its long stack of equally prominent advanced controls needs clearer hierarchy.
3. **The answer state is instantly recognizable.** A selected letter, red error mark, and green correct mark work at a glance. LSAT U should also state the result in words and preserve first versus later answers.
4. **Written explanations have real teaching value.** Separate contributors can frame the same gap differently, and every option can get a reason. Joshua can provide a canonical explanation plus an optional teacher note without recording videos.
5. **Avoid metrics without a question they answer.** The dashboard's broad progress percentage and response-popularity pills look precise but do not explain what the student should do next. Prioritize an actionable diagnosis with visible attempt count.

### LSAT Demon layout and interaction findings

1. **The rail has two useful levels.** A labeled primary item stays short while its current section expands locally. This is closer to Joshua's desired side rail than a long flat list. At laptop width, the expanded nav remains readable, but local submenus should cap at a few items and leave long taxonomies to the page.
2. **Focused work sheds the dashboard shell.** A question or lesson opens a dedicated reading surface. The question's stimulus, choices, and explanation remain in context. LSAT U can use this separation between finding work and doing work.
3. **Revealing the right answer is its own action.** Completed review retained the selected wrong choice and let me explicitly highlight the correct one. That protects the record of the student's first thinking. LSAT U should use words as well as red and green.
4. **The checklist asks for reasoning rather than a reaction.** It asks why the right answer works, why it was rejected, and how it could have been predicted, then asks what words make the chosen wrong answer wrong and why it was attractive. A text-first LSAT U journal can make those prompts optional but specific.
5. **Two modes communicate a teaching choice.** Prediction Mode adds process scaffolding; Test Mode removes it. LSAT U should name the learning mode and preserve the mode on each attempt so analytics do not silently pool unlike work.
6. **Status and related artifacts make history useful.** Continue, review-needed, messages, notes, and bookmarks belong beside attempts. The same metadata can drive My Work, Review, and Progress without three independent records.
7. **Several polished surfaces overstate the importance of sparse data.** The dashboard's large score gauges and analytics zero rows look finished but say little on a new account. LSAT U should let work and instruction lead until measurements are meaningful.
8. **The visual system is consistent but sometimes cramped.** Navy rail, lavender secondary text, white space, subdued dividers, and clear green/red answer states support scanning. The three-column review compresses explanation text on a laptop; an adjustable pane or mobile stacked layout needs testing.

### Proposed page anatomy and visual rules

- **Student rail:** Home, My Work, Practice, Review, Progress, Library, Schedule. Use text labels and consistent icons. The active parent can reveal at most a few local links, while longer topic or question-type lists live in a searchable page. Collapse the rail only at narrow breakpoints and keep a visible way to reopen it.
- **Teacher rail:** Students, Plans, Library, Schedule, with View student experience readily available. Inside Library, Bootcamps and Activities are separate tabs; authoring, preview, and assignment controls remain within those tabs.
- **Page header:** title, one sentence explaining the page's job when needed, one primary action, then local tabs with stable URLs. Keep destructive and secondary controls out of the primary action slot.
- **First screen:** a next step or a compact summary plus a few relevant rows. Show a count on Review or My Work only if it reflects complete coverage. Empty states name the missing data and give a real next action, without contradictory onboarding claims.
- **Question workspace:** reading area and answer area stay stable; explanation, note, and Joshua feedback share a contextual panel. Preserve the original response and mode. A deliberate reveal action and text labels accompany colors. Make the panel adjustable or stack it below the question at narrower widths.
- **Visual density:** generous line height for LSAT text, restrained card borders, and one accent for actions. Use high contrast for answer states and focus rings. Allow compact table mode for Joshua and advanced students, but keep the default queue readable without sideways scanning.
- **Micro feedback:** distinguish Selected, Correct, Incorrect, Flagged, Review due, Reviewed, and Teacher selected in words. A mode label, source, time condition, and attempt count travel with each result. Write the denominator beside every percentage.
- **No new video dependency:** prefer written worked reasoning, concise skill exercises, question-specific notes, and existing material. Offer an existing video as an optional explanation format if one already exists.

## Cross-platform design for LSAT U

**Recommended information architecture, based on Joshua's accepted mergers:** a seven-item student rail holds Home, My Work, Practice, Review, Progress, Library, and Schedule. My Work contains To do, Completed, Notes & recaps. Joshua confirmed on 2026-09-28 that Practice contains Drills and a distinct Practice Exams area. Practice Exams preserves its approved Overview landing with two start actions and Unfinished exams and sections, plus All Exams and Attempts. Saved sets stay with Drills. Timed start is deliberate. Review contains To review, Wrong answers, Flagged, and teacher-selected items as filters or tabs. Progress contains Overview, History, and topic detail. Library contains teaching material and activities. Source inspection after the browser audit found that the teacher already has four rooms, Today, Inbox, Library, and Insights, and Library already has Bootcamps and Activities tabs. Preserve that shell and add an obvious View student experience action. The earlier proposed replacement teacher rail of Students, Plans, Library, and Schedule is superseded by this finding. Validate the final student labels against actual routes and tasks before removing destinations.

**One end-to-end student path to design first:** Home says what Joshua wants done next and why. My Work shows the assigned activity with time and completion state. Practice starts in a named training or test mode, with an Inspect option for a question that should not count as an attempt. The question workspace preserves first reasoning and optional blind review. Review queues the miss, flag, skip, or teacher-selected question with a reason; its checklist asks for the student's diagnosis before Joshua's written guidance. Progress shows the pattern and links to a suitable drill. A future session page ties the assigned prep to Joshua's recap and follow-up. This path would make the smaller navigation genuinely easier, instead of just hiding existing links.

**Where LSAT U can be better:** Joshua can make a priority personal and explain the reasoning behind it; competitors mostly rely on automatic ranks. A recap can connect the exact teaching moment to the student's original attempt, next drill, and next session. Written, focused Activities can teach one reasoning operation without a video catalog. Teacher and student can share the same question identity and context rather than exchange vague PT references. These advantages depend on the shared attempt/flag/assignment coverage contract described above.
