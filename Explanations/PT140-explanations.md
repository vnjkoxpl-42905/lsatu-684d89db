## S3 · Q10 — Must be True

### Stimulus
Whenever you subcontract the manufacturing of a product, you lose some control over the quality of that product. We do subcontract some manufacturing, but only with companies that maintain complete control over the quality of the products they supply.

### Prompt
10. Which one of the following can be properly inferred from the company president's statements?

### Written
**Statements**
- Subcontracting manufacturing causes loss of some quality control.
- The company only subcontracts with companies that maintain complete control over quality.
**Evaluate**
- From the statements, we can infer that subcontractors do not subcontract further and still maintain complete control.
**Goal**
- Find the inference that follows from the two conditionals.
**Answer choices**
**A. When the president's company subcontracts manufacturing of a product, it does not allow the subcontractor to further subcontract manufacturing of that product.** — Correct (67% of students)
This follows from the company's requirement that subcontractors maintain complete control.

**B. Companies that subcontract the manufacturing of products are often disappointed in the quality of those products.** — Out of Scope: disappointed (5% of students)
No such claim is supported.

**C. The company president insists on having as much control as possible over the quality of the company's products.** — Too Strong: insists on max control (22% of students)
This is contradicted by subcontracting.

**D. When consumers know that a product has been manufactured by a subcontractor, they are generally dubious about the product's quality.** — Out of Scope: consumers / dubious (2% of students)
Consumers are not discussed.

**E. When a company manufactures some products in-house and subcontracts the manufacturing of others, the products made in-house will be of uniformly better quality.** — Out of Scope: in-house vs subcontract / Too Strong: uniformly better (5% of students)
This is not supported.

## S3 · Q11 — Sufficient Assumption

### Stimulus
Secondary school students achieve broad mastery of the curriculum if they are taught with methods appropriate to their learning styles and they devote significant effort to their studies. Thus, if such broad mastery is not achieved by the students in a particular secondary school, those students are not being taught with methods appropriate to their learning styles.

### Prompt
11. The conclusion can be properly drawn if which one of the following is assumed?

### Written
**Conclusion**
- If broad mastery is not achieved, then the students were not taught with methods appropriate to their learning styles.
**Evidence**
- Broad mastery follows from appropriate methods and significant effort.
**Evaluate**
- The author is moving from `Appropriate Methods AND Effort → Broad Mastery` to `No Broad Mastery → No Appropriate Methods`.
- That move is invalid unless we rule out the possibility that lack of effort caused the failure.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that guarantees students with appropriate methods also devote significant effort, so the lack of broad mastery must be due to the teaching method.
**Answer choices**
**A. As long as secondary school students are taught with methods appropriate to their learning styles, they will devote significant effort to their studies.** — Correct (50% of students)
This closes the gap. If appropriate methods guarantee effort, then a failure to achieve broad mastery cannot be blamed on low effort, so the author can conclude the methods were inappropriate.

**B. Even if secondary school students are taught with methods appropriate to their learning styles, they will not achieve broad mastery of the curriculum if they do not devote significant effort to their studies.** — Reversal / Out of Scope (17% of students)
This restates part of the stimulus and does not bridge the gap.

**C. Secondary school students do not achieve broad mastery of the curriculum if they are not taught with methods appropriate to their learning styles.** — Reversal of Conclusion (21% of students)
This runs in the wrong direction.

**D. Teaching secondary school students with methods appropriate to their learning styles does not always result in broad mastery of the curriculum by those students.** — Too Weak / Nothing New (2% of students)
This is already consistent with the stimulus and does not prove the conclusion.

**E. Secondary school students who devote significant effort to their studies do not always achieve broad mastery of the curriculum.** — Too Weak / Nothing New (10% of students)
This also does not close the gap.

## S3 · Q12 — Main Conclusion

### Stimulus
Consumer advocate: Even if one can of fruit or vegetables weighs more than another, the heavier can does not necessarily contain more food. Canned fruits and vegetables are typically packed in water, which can make up more than half the total weight of the can's contents. And nothing stops unscrupulous canning companies from including more water per can than others include.

### Prompt
12. Which one of the following most accurately expresses the conclusion drawn in the consumer advocate's argument?

### Written
**Conclusion**
- The heavier of two cans of fruit or vegetables does not necessarily contain more food than the lighter of the two cans contains.
**Evidence**
- Canned fruit and vegetables are typically packed in water.
- Some companies include more water than others.
**Evaluate**
- The first sentence is the conclusion; the later sentences support it by showing why weight does not tell you how much food is inside.
**Goal**
- Pick the answer that restates the first sentence.
**Answer choices**
**A. The heavier of two cans of fruit or vegetables does not necessarily contain more food than the lighter of the two cans contains.** — Correct (78% of students)
This is the exact conclusion.

**B. The weight of the water in a can of fruit or vegetables can be more than half the total weight of the can's contents.** — Premise (3% of students)
This is support, not the conclusion.

**C. Nothing stops unscrupulous canning companies from including more water per can than others include.** — Premise / Last Sentence Trap (5% of students)
This is another supporting claim, not the main point.

**D. Some canning companies include less food in cans of a given weight than others include.** — Unstated Inference (5% of students)
This can be inferred, but it is not what the author concludes.

**E. The heavier of two cans of fruits or vegetables may include more water than the lighter of the two cans contains.** — Unstated Inference (9% of students)
This is also inferable, not the conclusion.

## S3 · Q13 — Most Supported

### Stimulus
Several three-year-olds who had learned to count to ten were trying to learn their telephone numbers. Although each child was familiar with the names of all the digits, no child could remember his or her phone number. Their teacher then taught each child a song whose lyrics contained his or her phone number. By the end of the day the children could remember their telephone numbers.

### Prompt
13. The situation described above best illustrates which one of the following propositions?

### Written
**Situation**
- Some 3 year olds knew the digits 0 through 9 but could not remember their phone numbers.
- The teacher taught the numbers in a song.
- By the end of the day, the children could remember the numbers.
**Evaluate**
- The song appears to have been the difference-maker that helped the children remember the order of familiar words.
**Goal**
- Pick the safest generalization that matches this example without overclaiming.
**Answer choices**
**A. There are some things that children cannot learn without the aid of songs.** — Too Strong (2% of students)
This says songs are necessary, but the example only shows songs were helpful here.

**B. Familiarity with a concept is not always sufficient for knowing the words used to express it.** — 2nd Half Doesn't Match (9% of students)
The children were already familiar with the digit names, so this does not fit the example.

**C. Mnemonic devices such as songs are better than any other method for memorizing numbers.** — Too Strong (4% of students)
This makes an unsupported comparison to every other method.

**D. Children can learn to count without understanding the meaning of numbers.** — 2nd Half Doesn't Match (5% of students)
The passage does not show that the children lacked understanding of number meaning.

**E. Songs are useful in helping children remember the order in which familiar words occur.** — Correct (80% of students)
This matches the example without overclaiming.

## S3 · Q14 — Sufficient Assumption

### Stimulus
Some theorists argue that literary critics should strive to be value-neutral in their literary criticism. These theorists maintain that by exposing the meaning of literary works without evaluating them, critics will enable readers to make their own judgments about the works' merits. But literary criticism cannot be completely value-neutral. Thus, some theorists are mistaken about what is an appropriate goal for literary criticism.

### Prompt
14. The argument's conclusion follows logically if which one of the following is assumed?

### Written
**Conclusion**
- Some theorists are mistaken about what is an appropriate goal for literary criticism.
- More specifically, critics should not strive to be value-neutral.
**Evidence**
- Literary criticism cannot be completely value-neutral.
**Evaluate**
- The author is converting `cannot be completely value-neutral` into `should not strive to be value-neutral`.
- That requires a rule connecting impossibility with bad advice.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that lets the author infer the stronger normative conclusion from the descriptive premise.
**Answer choices**
**A. Any critic who is able to help readers make their own judgments about literary works' merits should strive to produce value-neutral criticism.** — Bad Conclusion Match (11% of students)
This points toward the opposite conclusion.

**B. If it is impossible to produce completely value-neutral literary criticism, then critics should not even try to be value-neutral.** — Correct (60% of students)
This gives the needed bridge from the premise to the conclusion.

**C. Critics are more likely to provide criticisms of the works they like than to provide criticisms of the works they dislike.** — Unrelated to Goal (5% of students)
This says nothing about value-neutrality.

**D. The less readers understand the meaning of a literary work, the less capable they will be of evaluating that work's merits.** — Unrelated to Goal (11% of students)
This is about readers, not the critics' goal.

**E. Critics who try to avoid rendering value judgments about the works they consider tend to influence readers' judgments less than other critics do.** — Unrelated to Goal (14% of students)
This is a weak comparison, not the required rule.

## S3 · Q15 — Parallel Flaw

### Stimulus
Amoebas, like human beings, generally withdraw from stimuli that cause them physical damage. Humans do this because such stimuli cause them pain. Thus all microscopic organisms must also be capable of feeling pain.

### Prompt
15. Which one of the following exhibits flawed reasoning most similar to that exhibited by the argument above?

### Written
**Conclusion**
- All microscopic organisms must also be capable of feeling pain.
**Evidence**
- Amoebas, like human beings, withdraw from damaging stimuli.
- Humans do this because such stimuli cause pain.
**Evaluate**
- The argument treats one member of a broader group as though it shares the same explanation as a comparison case.
- It also generalizes from a narrower group to a broader one.
**Goal**
- Find the answer with the same comparison flaw and the same broad-to-narrow mistake.
**Answer choices**
**A. Poets, like people under hypnosis, frequently use language in odd, incomprehensible ways. People under hypnosis do this because their inhibitions are lower than those of most people. Thus all artists must have lower inhibitions than most people have.** — Correct (67% of students)
This matches both flaws: it compares poets to hypnotized people and then generalizes from poets to all artists.

**B. Like nonprofit organizations, corporations usually provide some free public services. Nonprofit organizations do this solely because of their members' desire to make the world a better place. Thus this is probably also the main motive of most corporations.** — Bad Conclusion Match (13% of students)
This is a probabilistic conclusion, not the same kind of all-claim.

**C. Most professional athletes practice regularly for the same reason. Professional boxers spend several hours a day practicing in order to excel in competition. Thus professional skaters probably also practice in order to excel in competition.** — Bad Conclusion/Evidence Match (4% of students)
The premise is more general than the conclusion.

**D. Predatory birds, like many predatory animals, are generally solitary hunters. Some predatory mammals hunt alone because there is not enough food to support a pack of them in one area. Thus hawks, which are predatory birds, probably hunt alone.** — Bad Conclusion/Evidence Match (5% of students)
This also reverses the category relationship.

**E. Hiking trails in British Columbia, like those in New Mexico, are concentrated in mountainous regions. In New Mexico this is partly because low-lying areas are too hot and arid for comfortable hiking. Thus hikers must also feel less comfortable hiking in low-lying areas of British Columbia.** — Bad Evidence Match (10% of students)
This does not preserve the original comparison structure.

## S3 · Q16 — Strengthen

### Stimulus
Zoologist: In the Lake Champlain area, as the North American snowshoe hare population grows, so do the populations of its predators. As predator numbers increase, the hares seek food in more heavily forested areas, which contain less food, and so the hare population declines. Predator populations thus decline, the hare population starts to increase, and the cycle begins again. Yet these facts alone cannot explain why populations of snowshoe hares everywhere behave simultaneously in this cyclical way. Since the hare population cycle is well correlated with the regular cycle of sunspot activity, that activity is probably a causal factor as well.

### Prompt
16. Each of the following, if true, supports the zoologist's reasoning except:

### Written
**Conclusion**
- Sunspot activity is probably a causal factor in the hare cycle.
**Evidence**
- Hare population cycles correlate with the sunspot cycle.
- The local predator-prey dynamic alone cannot explain why hare populations everywhere cycle simultaneously.
**Evaluate**
- This is an explain-a-curious-fact strengthen question.
- Four answers should make sunspots more plausible or weaken an alternate explanation.
**Goal**
- Find the one answer that does not help.
**Answer choices**
**A. Reproduction in predator populations increases when sunspot activity indirectly affects hormonal processes associated with reproduction.** — Strengthens (9% of students)
This makes sunspot causation more plausible.

**B. Local weather patterns that can affect species' population changes can occur both in the presence of sunspot activity and in its absence.** — Correct (72% of students)
This points to an alternate explanation and does not support the sunspot causal story.

**C. Brighter light during sunspot activity subtly but significantly improves the ability of predators to detect and capture hares.** — Strengthens (7% of students)
This supplies a causal mechanism.

**D. The variation from cycle to cycle in the magnitude of the highs and lows in snowshoe hare populations is highly correlated with variations from cycle to cycle in the intensity of highs and lows in sunspot activity.** — Strengthens (5% of students)
This tightens the correlation.

**E. Sunspot activity is correlated with increases and decreases in the nutritional value of vegetation eaten by the hares.** — Strengthens (7% of students)
This also supplies a causal mechanism.

## S3 · Q17 — Most Supported

### Stimulus
Science teacher: In any nation, a flourishing national scientific community is essential to a successful economy. For such a community to flourish requires that many young people become excited enough about science that they resolve to become professional scientists. Good communication between scientists and the public is necessary to spark that excitement.

### Prompt
17. The science teacher's statements provide the most support for which one of the following?

### Written
**Statements**
- Successful economy requires a flourishing national scientific community.
- Flourishing scientific community requires many young people excited enough to become professional scientists.
- Many young people excited enough to become professional scientists requires good communication between scientists and the public.
**Evaluate**
- This is a conditional chain.
- The safest support comes from the contrapositive of the whole chain.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that follows from the chain without reversing or overstrengthening it.
**Answer choices**
**A. If scientists communicate with the public, many young people will become excited enough about science to resolve to become professional scientists.** — Reversed Logic (21% of students)
This goes backward from communication to excitement.

**B. The extent to which a national scientific community flourishes depends principally on the number of young people who become excited enough about science to resolve to become professional scientists.** — Relative vs. Absolute / One-Claim Support (24% of students)
This is too comparative and not fully supported.

**C. No nation can have a successful economy unless at some point scientists have communicated well with the public.** — Correct (43% of students)
This is the contrapositive of the chain.

**D. It is essential to any nation's economy that most of the young people in that nation who are excited about science become professional scientists.** — Too Strong: Most (6% of students)
The stimulus only says many, not most.

**E. An essential component of success in any scientific endeavor is good communication between the scientists involved in that endeavor and the public.** — Too Strong: any scientific endeavor (5% of students)
This overextends beyond the given chain.

## S3 · Q18 — Flaw

### Stimulus
A recent magazine article argued that most companies that do not already own videoconferencing equipment would be wasting their money if they purchased it. However, this is clearly not true. In a recent survey of businesses that have purchased such equipment, most of the respondents stated that the videoconferencing equipment was well worth its cost.

### Prompt
18. The reasoning in the argument is flawed in that the argument

### Written
**Conclusion**
- Most companies would not be wasting their money if they purchased videoconferencing equipment.
**Evidence**
- In a survey of businesses that have purchased videoconferencing equipment, most respondents said it was worth the cost.
**Evaluate**
- The argument assumes the sample is representative of most companies.
- It also treats a self-selected group of purchasers as if it represents the whole market.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that identifies the unrepresentative sample.
**Answer choices**
**A. concludes that something is worth its cost merely on the grounds that many businesses have purchased it** — Bad Evidence Match (8% of students)
The argument uses survey responses, not mere purchase volume.

**B. takes a condition sufficient to justify purchasing costly equipment to be necessary in order for the cost of the purchase to be justified** — Not Necessary vs. Sufficient (11% of students)
There is no conditional logic here.

**C. rejects a position merely on the grounds that an inadequate argument has been given for it** — Not Unproven vs. Proven False / Bad Evidence Match (5% of students)
The argument uses survey evidence, not just an inadequate opposing argument.

**D. relies on a sample that it is reasonable to suppose is unrepresentative of the group about which it draws its conclusion** — Correct (61% of students)
This is the sampling flaw.

**E. confuses the cost of an item with its value to the purchaser** — Doesn't Confuse (15% of students)
The argument already compares cost and value correctly.

## S3 · Q19 — Necessary Assumption

### Stimulus
Auditor: XYZ, a construction company, purchased 20 new trucks 3 years ago, and there is no record of any of those trucks being sold last year. Records indicate, however, that XYZ sold off all of its diesel-powered trucks last year. We can thus conclude that none of the 20 trucks purchased 3 years ago were diesel powered.

### Prompt
19. Which one of the following is an assumption required by the auditor's reasoning?

### Written
**Conclusion**
- None of the 20 trucks purchased 3 years ago were diesel powered.
**Evidence**
- None of the 20 trucks purchased 3 years ago were sold last year.
- All of the company's diesel trucks were sold last year.
**Evaluate**
- The argument assumes those 20 trucks were still owned last year.
- Otherwise, a diesel truck could have been sold before last year.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that blocks that escape route.
**Answer choices**
**A. All of the trucks that XYZ sold last year were diesel powered.** — Too Strong: all (21% of students)
The stimulus only says all diesels were sold last year.

**B. XYZ did not purchase any used trucks 3 years ago.** — Out of Scope: used trucks (4% of students)
Used trucks do not matter here.

**C. XYZ did not purchase any new trucks since it purchased the 20 trucks 3 years ago.** — Out of Scope: other new trucks (13% of students)
This does not affect the conclusion about the 20 trucks.

**D. None of the 20 trucks was sold before last year.** — Correct (57% of students)
This is the needed assumption.

**E. XYZ no longer owns any trucks that it purchased more than 3 years ago.** — Out of Scope: more than 3 years ago (5% of students)
This is irrelevant to the argument.

## S3 · Q21 — Weaken

### Stimulus
Prolonged exposure to sulfur fumes permanently damages one's sense of smell. In one important study, 100 workers from sulfur-emitting factories and a control group of 100 workers from other occupations were asked to identify a variety of chemically reproduced scents, including those of foods, spices, and flowers. On average, the factory workers successfully identified 10 percent of the scents compared to 50 percent for the control group.

### Prompt
21. Each of the following, if true, weakens the argument except:

### Written
**Conclusion**
- Prolonged exposure to sulfur fumes permanently damages one's sense of smell.
**Evidence**
- Factory workers scored 10% on the smell test, while the control group scored 50%.
**Evaluate**
- This is a causal-explanation argument.
- A weakener will usually supply an alternate explanation or undermine the causal story.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that does not weaken.
**Answer choices**
**A. The chemicals used in the study closely but not perfectly reproduced the corresponding natural scents.** — Correct (45% of students)
This does not explain the difference between the two groups.

**B. The subjects in the study were tested in the environments where they usually work.** — Alternative Explanation (21% of students)
This could explain the lower factory scores.

**C. Most members of the control group had participated in several earlier studies that involved the identification of scents.** — Alternative Explanation (9% of students)
This could explain the control group's better performance.

**D. Every sulfur-emitting factory with workers participating in the study also emits other noxious fumes.** — Alternative Explanation (16% of students)
This suggests a different cause.

**E. Because of the factories' locations, the factory workers were less likely than those in the control group to have been exposed to many of the scents used in the study.** — Alternative Explanation (9% of students)
This also gives a non-sulfur explanation.

Principle: Anyone who has more than one overdue book out on loan from the library at the same time must be fined if some of the overdue books are not children's books and that person has previously been fined for overdue books.

Application: Since three of the books that Kessler currently has out on loan from the library are overdue, Kessler must be fined.

**Principle**
- More than one overdue book

- Kessler must be fined.

- Kessler has 3 overdue books.

- The application needs the other two principle conditions as well.

**D. Kessler was fined by the library several times in the past for overdue books, but none of the overdue books for which Kessler was fined were children's books.** — Doesn't Establish #2 (10% of students)

## S3 · Q24 — Parallel

### Stimulus
If the concrete is poured while the ground is wet, it will not form a solid foundation. If the concrete does not form a solid foundation, it will either settle unevenly or crack. So if the concrete settles evenly, either it was poured while the ground was dry or it will crack.

### Prompt
24. Which one of the following arguments is most closely parallel in its reasoning to the reasoning in the argument above?

### Written
**Premise 1**
- Wet ground → no solid foundation
**Premise 2**
- No solid foundation → settle unevenly or crack
**Conclusion**
- Settle evenly → dry ground or crack
**Evaluate**
- The argument chains two conditionals and preserves the `or` in the conclusion.
**Goal**
- Find the answer with the same structure.
**Answer choices**
**A. The film will not be properly exposed if the camera is not working properly. If the film is not properly exposed, then the photograph will be either blurred or dark. So if the photograph is not blurred, either the camera is working properly or the photograph will be dark.** — Correct (77% of students)
This matches the chained conditionals and the `or` outcome in the conclusion.

**B. If the camera is working properly, the photograph will not be blurred. The photograph will be blurred if the film is either not properly exposed or not properly developed. So if the camera is working properly, the film will be both properly exposed and properly developed.** — Bad Premise / Conclusion Match (9% of students)
The `or` is in the wrong place and the conclusion has `and`.

**C. The photograph will either be blurred or dark if the camera is not working properly. This photograph is not blurred, so if the photograph is not dark, the camera is working properly.** — Bad Evidence / Conclusion Match (5% of students)
This lacks the needed chain and `or` structure.

**D. If the camera is working properly, the film will be properly exposed. If either the film is properly exposed or corrections are made during the developing process, the photograph will not be dark. So if the camera is working properly, the photograph will not be dark.** — Bad Evidence / Conclusion Match (5% of students)
The `or` is in the trigger rather than the conclusion.

**E. The camera will work properly only if the film is properly exposed. But the film cannot be properly exposed if there is either not enough or too much light. So the camera will not work properly if there is either too much or not enough light.** — Bad Evidence / Conclusion Match (4% of students)
The structure does not match the original.

## S3 · Q25 — Strengthen

### Stimulus
New evidence indicates that recent property development bordering a national park has not adversely affected the park's wildlife. On the contrary, a comparison of the most recent survey of the park's wildlife with one conducted just prior to the development shows that the amount of wildlife has in fact increased over the intervening decade. Moreover, the park's resources can support its current wildlife populations without strain.

### Prompt
25. Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the argument?

### Written
**Conclusion**
- The recent property development bordering the national park has not adversely affected the park's wildlife.
**Evidence**
- The amount of wildlife has increased since the development.
- The park's resources can support current wildlife populations.
**Evaluate**
- The argument assumes the increase is real and not just a measurement artifact.
- It also assumes no species declined even if total wildlife increased.
**Goal**
- Find the answer that removes the best objection and makes the conclusion more plausible.
**Answer choices**
**A. While both surveys found the same species of animals in the park, the more recent survey found greater numbers of animals belonging to each species.** — Correct (57% of students)
This rules out the possibility that the total increase hid declines in individual species.

**B. The more recent survey was taken in the summer, when the diversity of wildlife in the park is at its greatest.** — No Impact (5% of students)
This does not show the development was harmless.

**C. Migration of wildlife into the park from the adjacent developing areas has increased animal populations to levels beyond those that the resources of the park could have supported a decade ago.** — No Impact (22% of students)
This does not strengthen the conclusion.

**D. The most recent techniques for surveying wildlife are better at locating difficult-to-find animals than were older techniques.** — Weakens (7% of students)
This suggests the increase may be a measurement artifact.

## S1 · Q1 — Sufficient Assumption

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
Several years ago, most of one country's large banks failed and were taken over by a government agency. The agency is now selling these banks, aiming to strengthen the banking system in the process. But the banking system will not be strengthened if the former owners of these banks buy them back. So the agency is unlikely to achieve its goal, since _______ .

### Prompt
1. The conclusion of the argument is properly drawn if which one of the following completes the passage?

### Written (single style)
**Argument breakdown**
- **Conclusion:** The agency is unlikely to achieve its goal (i.e. the banking system will not be strengthened).
- **Evidence:** If the former owners of these banks buy them back, the banking system will not be strengthened.
- If an argument gives us a conditional premise `X → Y` and then concludes `So, Y.`, then what is the missing idea?
- "We're missing this:"
- `X.`
- We know **if** the former owners buy back, the banking system will not be strengthened.
- Our author concludes the banking system will not be strengthened.
- **Goal:** So, we want to hear that the former owners will buy these banks back.
**Answer choices**
**A. the agency may be unable to sell some of the banks** — Weak / Unrelated to Goal (3% of students)
We'll almost never see weak wording like "may" in the correct answer to Sufficient Assumption, since we're looking for an answer that guarantees the conclusion is true (follows logically / properly drawn / properly inferred).

Since we're trying to prove the idea that "the banking system will not be strengthened", and since we were provided a law that says `if X →` banking system won't be strengthened

We need an answer to tell us "X", which is that "the former owners will buy back these banks".

**B. a single company could buy more than one of the banks** — Weak / Unrelated to Goal (1% of students)
We'll almost never see weak wording like "could" in the correct answer to Sufficient Assumption, since we're looking for an answer that guarantees the conclusion is true (follows logically / properly drawn / properly inferred).

Since we're trying to prove the idea that "the banking system will not be strengthened", and since we were provided a law that says `if X →` banking system won't be strengthened

We need an answer to tell us "X", which is that "the former owners will buy back these banks".

**C. the country's overall economy is not much stronger than it was when the large banks failed** — Out of Scope: overall economy (3% of students)
The correct answer on Sufficient Assumption will almost never contain wording we haven't already used in the passage, since it's just trying to patch together loose ends (not create a new loose end by introducing new wording like "overall economy").

**D. the banks sold by the agency will be financially weaker than the country's other banks for some time** — Irrelevant Comparison (2% of students)
We're only here to judge / prove whether the banking system will / won't be strengthened.

Wording like "these banks will be weaker than other banks for some time" is a very wishy-washy idea. We need definitive understanding that the banking system isn't strengthened.

**E. all of the bidders for the banks are their former owners** — Correct (92% of students)
We were looking to hear that "the former owner of these banks will buy them back". If we knew that, it would prove the idea that the banking system will not be strengthened.

But since the actual conclusion is just "the agency is unlikely to achieve its goal" we need to prove that "the banking system is likely not going to be strengthened".

If all the bidders for the banks are former owners, then it's likely that the former owners of the banks are going to buy them back, so it's likely that the banking system will not be strengthened.

## S1 · Q3 — Flaw

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
Peter: Recent evidence suggests that moderate alcohol consumption has certain beneficial effects on health. In particular, alcohol creates an inhospitable environment in the human body for certain bacteria that can cause illness. Thus, alcohol consumption is, on balance, beneficial.

### Prompt
3. Which one of the following most accurately expresses a flaw in the reasoning in Peter's argument?

### Written (single style)
**Argument breakdown**
- **Conclusion:** Alcohol consumption is, on balance, beneficial.
- **Evidence:** According to recent evidence, moderate alcohol consumption has certain beneficial effects on health (such as killing off some bacteria that can cause illness).
- **Evaluate:** Given That ... moderate alcohol consumption seems to have some beneficial effects How Could We Argue That ... alcohol consumption is not beneficial overall?
- We'd probably have to say that the author is failing to talk about the downsides of alcohol consumption (empty sugars? raises blood pressure? hurts liver?), and that the downsides might outweigh the "certain beneficial effects" such as killing off some harmful bacteria that would be living in your mouth / throat.
- **Goal:** Look for an answer that says the author is only presenting part of the story. If we're trying to conclude that something is "on balance / overall" beneficial, then we should have laid out all the pros, all the cons, and established that the pros outweigh the cons.
**Answer choices**
**A. It takes for granted that people choose to consume alcohol because they believe it is beneficial to their health.** — Out of Scope: because they believe (2% of students)
The author hasn't mentioned anything about what people do / don't believe about alcohol or speculated in any way about why people drink, so the author hasn't committed to any assumptions about why people choose to drink alcohol.

If you negated this idea and said "people don't drink because they believe alcohol is beneficial", the author's argument wouldn't be weakened. She'd just say, "Cool, I never said that's WHY they drink; it just so happens to be an effect of them drinking."

**B. It draws a comparison based on popular belief rather than on scientific opinion.** — Out of Scope: popular belief (1% of students)
Does the author draw a comparison? Nope. Is there reference to a popular belief? Nope. Is there a scientific opinion? Yeah, we could probably consider "recent evidence suggests" a scientific opinion.

So all three parts of this answer don't match.

Appealing to people's belief rather than actual scientific data is a famous flaw known as Inappropriate Appeal (to Opinion).

**C. It fails to consider methods of achieving the same beneficial effects that do not involve alcohol.** — Not an Objection (13% of students)
Does it hurt the author's argument if we say, "Yo, my dude -- there are other ways to make your body an inhospitable environment than consuming alcohol", the author would just say "Sure, I know. I never said alcohol was the only substance that had these beneficial effects, just that it is a substance with these beneficial effects."

**D. It draws a conclusion about alcohol consumption in general from a premise about moderate alcohol consumption.** — Correct (74% of students)
Does the author draw a conclusion about alcohol consumption in general? Sure, the conclusion just says "alcohol consumption is beneficial".

Is the author's premise about moderate alcohol consumption?

Yes -- she says that moderate alcohol consumption has certain beneficial effects on health. But maybe light alcohol consumption or heavy alcohol consumption doesn't have those benefits. (perhaps light alcohol consumption is not enough to render your insides inhospitable to bacteria, whereas heavy alcohol consumption starts to be very damaging to your heart/liver)

By shifting from evidence about a subtype of alcohol consumption to a conclusion about all types of alcohol consumption, the author is making a Sampling assumption that what is true of moderate consumption is also true of light / heavy consumption.

We could object to the author by saying, "Even though moderate alcohol consumption can have beneficial effects, light alcohol consumption is totally neutral and heavy alcohol consumption is very damaging. Thus, we can't really just generalize that alcohol consumption is, on balance, beneficial."

**E. It fails to consider that alcohol may have no effect on many bacteria that cause illness in human beings.** — Not an Objection (10% of students)
The author didn't promise that alcohol killed 100% of bacteria, so pointing out that it has no effect on many bacteria isn't an objection.

It might even be that alcohol doesn't kill off the good bacteria that's part of a healthy gut. That would actually help the author's case.

Something doesn't need to be a perfect/total solution to be a beneficial action.

## S1 · Q4 — Most Supported

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
Consultant: Children taught using innovative new educational methods learn to think more creatively than children taught using rote methods such as drills, but they are less adept at memorizing large amounts of information. Most jobs at Grodex Corporation require the ability to think creatively but do not require a strong ability to memorize. So Grodex should probably conduct its employee-training seminars using the innovative methods, because _______ .

### Prompt
4. Which one of the following most logically completes the consultant's argument?

### Written (single style)
**Argument breakdown**
- **Conclusion (So):** Grodex should probably conduct its employee-training seminars using these innovative methods.
- **Evidence:** Most jobs at Grodex require thinking creatively but don't require being great at memorizing.
- Children taught using these innovative new educational methods learn to think more creatively than children taught using rote methods, but they are less adept at memorizing.
- **Evaluate:** The blank is prefaced by a Support Indicator, "because", so we know that we're supposed to pick an answer that would strengthen the claim before it.
- Our correct answer should make it more plausible that Grodex should use these new methods in employee-training.
- We already know that what the methods are good at (teaching creativity) is more important to most jobs at Grodex than what the methods are bad at (teaching memorization). So it makes sense that this author would see these methods as a good fit for most Grodex jobs.
- But let's be skeptical. What could be a reason not to implement these methods?
- maybe they're cost prohibitive
- maybe the people who conduct employee-training seminars don't have the ability to employ these innovative methods well
- maybe these methods work to promote creativity in whatever context the children are using them in, but that wouldn't transfer over to the type of creativity needed for a Grodex job
- maybe even though most jobs at Grodex value creativity more than memorization, the employee-training seminars are more about memorizing policies than about thinking creatively.
- **Goal:** This correct answer could really come from any number of directions. We want it to add another reason for Grodex to adopt these methods in its employee-training, or to rule out an objection to Grodex adopting these methods for employee-training.
**Answer choices**
**A. most of the employees at Grodex began in high school to learn the creative thinking skills that they later used on the job** — Weakens, if anything (3% of students)
This answer makes it seem like we're not dependent on these employee-training seminars for fostering creative thinking skills; the latter is taken care of for most employees by their high school education.

The whole selling point of implementing these new methods was their potential for teaching creative thinking, but if our workforce already comes to us with creative thinking skills, then that diminishes the incentive for using the new methods.

**B. corporations that conduct training seminars for employees using innovative educational methods are generally more successful than are corporations that do not conduct training seminars** — Weak Impact / Bad Comparison (5% of students)
This answer is pretty tempting at first, because it seems to be saying something positive about these new methods that would strengthen Grodex's desire to conduct its employee-training seminars using these methods.

However, a compelling comparison would be "corporations that conduct training seminars using these methods are generally more successful than are corporations that conduct training seminars using other methods."

That sort of comparison would make it seem like these innovative methods might be better than the other options.

But the comparison here is between using these methods and doing no training seminars at all.

If you're trying to tell me that I should buy a certain LSAT resource because it's "better than not studying at all", then that's a weak testimonial for that resource.

Moreover, the correlation in this answer choice might really just be telling us that more successful corporations have the budget and scale to run training seminars. (In other words, it's not that the training seminar is causing the success; the success is allowing the training seminar to happen.)

**C. less than half of the employees at Grodex regularly attend the company's training seminars** — No Impact (0% of students)
This answer feel more like a weakener, because if hardly any employees show up to these training seminars, then who cares what educational methods our seminars employ.

Technically, it's not really a reason for or against using these new methods.

**D. the effects of teaching methods in the education of adults are generally very similar to the effects of those methods in the education of children** — Correct (83% of students)
The author was Assuming a Similarity. She was thinking that, "If these new methods help children to think more creatively, then they will also help adults working for Grodex to think more creatively."

A potential objection to this conclusion could have been, "No, Grodex shouldn't use these methods. These are good for 5-10 year olds, but they wouldn't work on adults."

This answer rules out such an objection by telling us that the effects of these teaching methods are generally similar, whether it's kids or adults. So this gives Grodex reason to think that these methods generally would help an adult learn to think more creatively.

**E. knowing how to think creatively helps people to compensate for deficiencies in memorization skills** — No Impact (9% of students)
This might feel tempting, as though it's saying "Guess what --- the thing that these new methods are good for (improving creative thinking) is actually helpful for people who have bad memorization."

However, Grodex doesn't necessarily have any employees who have "deficiencies in memorization skills", so this doesn't really pose any benefit to Grodex.

We were just told that most jobs at Grodex don't require strong memorization skills. That's not the same as being told that most employees have weak memorization skills.

## S1 · Q5 — Main Conclusion

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
Essayist: If Earth's population continues to grow geometrically, then in a few centuries there will be ten people for every square meter (approximately one person per square foot) of Earth's surface. Some people have claimed that this will probably not be a problem, since humans will have learned by then how to colonize other planets. This would, however, be a temporary solution at best: if the population continues to double every 30 years, and if in the year 2500 half of Earth's population emigrated to Mars, then by the year 2530 Earth would be just as crowded as it had been before the emigration.

### Prompt
5. Which one of the following most accurately expresses the conclusion drawn in the essayist's argument?

### Written (single style)
**Argument breakdown**
- **Conclusion:** This would be a temporary solution at best. (i.e. colonizing other planets would only temporarily solve the problem of overpopulation on Earth)
- **Evidence:** Suppose the population is doubling every 30 years -- in the year 2500, half of Earth's population could emigrate to Mars, and you'd be right back to being just as crowded as you were on Earth by 2530.
- **Evaluate:** When we're doing Main Conclusion questions, we read the paragraph expecting that most of the time the Conclusion will appear earlier than the Evidence.
- In particular, we look out for the two most common patterns: - the Conclusion appears after a pivot word like "but, yet, however" - the Conclusion is the first sentence
- The first sentence of this paragraph is a mathematical fact. Facts aren't conclusions. Opinions are (supported Opinions, to be specific).
- When the second sentence begins "some people have claimed ...", we anticipate the classic form of a rebuttal argument: Some people say X. But / yet / however, they're wrong. Here's why.
- The third sentence begins with the "however" pivot we were anticipating. The author says that the solution these 'some people' are peddling is a temporary one, because if we're doubling ever 30 years, then losing half the population only solves the problem for 30 years.
- **Goal:** Look for an answer that sounds like the first clause of the final sentence: "colonizing other planets would be a temporary solution (to overcrowding) at best".
**Answer choices**
**A. If Earth's population continues to grow geometrically, then in a few centuries the population density of Earth's surface will be ten people per square meter.** — Background (3% of students)
This is just a factual, mathematical claim. It isn't supported by any reasons. Supporting reasons for this claim would sound mathematical:

why would the density be 10 people / sq. meter in this hypothetical?
because ... there are currently 8 billion people at a growth rate of 10% per year. The habitable land mass of Earth is 20 billion square meters. (etc.)

**B. Due to the continuing geometric growth of Earth's population, the problem of overpopulation of Earth will probably persist.** — Unstated (12% of students)
We're not trying to draw an Inference or call out an unstated Assumption on Main Conclusion. We're trying to point at the explicit claim that is the author's conclusion and then find an answer that matches.

This answer choice was never stated.

**C. If Earth's population continues to double every 30 years, and if at some point half of the population of Earth emigrated elsewhere, then after 30 years Earth would be just as crowded as it had been before the emigration.** — Last Idea Trap (11% of students)
The conclusion is the last claim in the paragraph only like 10% of the time on Main Conclusion questions, so be very suspicious any time you're picking an answer that says the last idea.

This is a premise, supporting our actual conclusion. There isn't any supporting reason offered for this claim. It's self-justifying math to say that, "If X doubles every 2 hours, then if you cut X in half, you'll be back to the original amount 2 hours later."

**D. The population of Earth's surface will probably continue to grow geometrically even if temporary solutions to population growth, such as colonizing other planets, are adopted.** — Unstated (16% of students)
We're not trying to draw an Inference or call out an unstated Assumption on Main Conclusion. We're trying to point at the explicit claim that is the author's conclusion and then find an answer that matches.

The idea in this answer choice was never stated.

**E. Learning how to colonize other planets would, at best, be a temporary solution to the overcrowding of Earth.** — Correct (58% of students)
This is the author's opinion, rebutting the "some people" in the previous claim.

Support is indicated by the colon, ":"

and if we were to ask, "why should we believe that it will be a temporary solution at best?", the final idea would sound like an answer -- "because if the population is doubling every 30 years, then shipping off half the population to another planet only buys you 30 years before you're right back where you started".

## S1 · Q6 — Strengthen

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
A recent taste test reveals that most people like low-fat chocolate ice cream as much as its full-fat counterpart. Previous tests with vanilla ice cream found that people tended to dislike low-fat versions, complaining of a harsher taste. Chemists point out that chocolate is a very complex flavor, requiring around 500 distinct chemical compounds to produce it. Hence, this complexity probably masks any difference in taste due to the lack of fat.

### Prompt
6. Which one of the following, if true, most strengthens the argument?

### Written (single style)
**Argument breakdown**
- **Conclusion:** The complexity of chocolate probably masks any difference in taste due to the lack of fat.
- **Evidence:** In a taste test, most people liked low-fat chocolate ice cream as much as full fat chocolate ice cream, whereas previous tests found that people tended to dislike low-fat vanilla ice cream, saying it tasted harsh.
- Chocolate is a very complex flavor, requiring around 500 distinct chemical compounds.
- **Evaluate:** Since we're trying to explain the meaning behind a Curious Comparison from two study results, we can use our Curious Fact / Explanation mindset.
- Curious Fact ... how come people like low-fat chocolate ice cream, but think low-fat vanilla ice cream has a harsh taste?
- Author's Explanation ... the complexity of chocolate masks the different taste of low-fat chocolate ice cream.
- The two pressure points with these types of arguments are - Alternate Explanations for the curious fact - the plausibility of the Author's Explanation
- How else could we explain the difference in reactions to the low-fat chocolate vs. the low-fat vanilla? - Maybe the low-fat chocolate was done by a very boutique ice cream maker (Ben & Jerry's) while the low-fat vanilla was done by Rite-Aid. - Maybe the vanilla taste test warned them in advance that they were going to taste something low-fat, whereas the chocolate test just let them try the samples first without warning them to expect something "worse".
- How plausible is the Author's Explanation? - well, we need to know that vanilla is not as complex a flavor (complexity can't be the Causal Difference-Maker here if it's not actually a difference) - we could appeal to other examples in which people preferred the healthier version of a complex flavor to the healthier version of a simpler flavor
- **Goal:** Find an answer that rules out an alternate explanation for the different reactions to low-fat chocolate vs. vanilla, or an answer that increases the plausibility of the causal explanation the author is selling, that "complexity is the causal difference-maker".
**Answer choices**
**A. Most people prefer full-fat chocolate ice cream to full-fat vanilla ice cream.** — Weakens, if anything (2% of students)
This answer is about full-fat ice cream, so it's not really helping us to investigate whether complexity of flavor is an effective way to mask the changed taste of a low-fat alternative.

If anything, this might sound like an Alternate Explanation "for the curious fact: \"they didn't prefer low-fat chocolate more than low-fat vanilla because the complexity masked the fat-less flavor; they preferred it because people just love chocolate so much that they'll be happy with any version of it.\""

**B. The subjects of the previous tests were not informed of the difference in fat content.** — Weaker than Correct Answer (10% of students)
This does strengthen somewhat, because it rules out the alternative explanation that people disliked the low-fat vanilla because they were warned in advance that it was low-fat (and so they mentally prepared themselves to hate it .... much in the same way if you told someone the glass of wine you poured came from a $100 bottle, they would prime their palate to love it).

But if you're trying to say, "The killer was Johnny", it has less strengthening effect to rule out an alternate explanation ("the killer wasn't Tanya") than it does to increase the plausibility of your explanation ("Johnny had a motive and a murder weapon").

Similarly, this answer has less impact than an answer that positively nudges us in favor of believing the complexity storyline.

**C. The more distinct compounds required to produce a flavor, the better people like it.** — Unclear Impact (18% of students)
"Which has more distinct compounds: vanilla or chocolate?"

We don't know. We were never told. So we have no way to know the potential impact of this answer on the conversation.

**D. Vanilla is known to be a significantly less complex flavor than chocolate.** — Correct (69% of students)
"This is establishing an assumption that is heavily insinuated but never said: chocolate is a more complex flavor than vanilla."

These Assumes a Difference arguments are annoying in the sense that the assumption is so obvious that it's hard to make your brain realize it was an assumption.

For example, "Tony is debating between buying a Prius and a Leaf. He should buy the Prius, since it has a bluetooth stereo."

What's being assumed? (among other things), the Leaf does not have a bluetooth stereo.

On Strengthen, Weaken, and Paradox (question stems asking, "Which answer, if it's true, does the most ..."), we love to see strong wording in our answer, so significantly less complex is very lovable.

**E. Most people are aware of the chemical complexities of different flavors.** — No Impact (1% of students)
It doesn't really make a difference to this argument whether most people are aware or unaware of the chemical complexity.

And if we're told they are aware of the chemical complexity, does that mean they like it? Does that mean that the complexity is effective at disguising the low-fat aftertaste?

We were never told that people like complexity. We can't really argue, "when they try low-fat chocolate, most of them are aware how chemically complex chocolate is ..... and that's why they don't mind the low-fat version?"

## S1 · Q8 — Most Supported

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
Many uses have been claimed for hypnosis, from combating drug addiction to overcoming common phobias. A recent experimental study helps illuminate the supposed connection between hypnosis and increased power of recall. A number of subjects listened to a long, unfamiliar piece of instrumental music. Under subsequent hypnosis, half the subjects were asked to recall salient passages from the musical piece and half were asked to describe scenes from "the film they had just viewed," despite their not having just seen a film. The study found that the subjects in the second group were equally confident and detailed in their movie recollections as the subjects in the first group were in their music recollections.

8. Which one of the following statements is most supported by the information above?

**Argument breakdown**
- **Information:** In a study, subjects listened to a long, unfamiliar piece of instrumental music.
- Once hypnotized, half the people were asked to remember parts of the musical piece and half were asked to describe scenes from the movie they had just seen (they hadn't seen a movie).
- Both groups were equally confident and detailed in their recollections.
- **Evaluate:** It feels like the correct answer will want to synthesize these research results. What does it mean that both groups are equally confident and detailed, even though one group is seemingly pulling from true memories (they did hear a piece of music, so when they're asked about it, they're answers might be legit) while the other group is seemingly pulling from 'false' memories (they didn't see a movie, so they're answers about the movie seem like confident, detailed lies).

**Answer choices**
**A. Many of the claims made on behalf of hypnosis are overstated.** — Too Strong: many (4% of students)

We could probably say that this claim is undermined by the results of this study. Half the subjects "remembered" scenes from a movie they never saw, so that doesn't sound like improved recall.

"Many" doesn't have a precise quantitative beginning, but it's safe think "at least 5".

But it's still possible that certain forms of hypnosis, in certain circumstances could significantly increase a person's power of recall. Maybe hypnosis has the power to elicit harder-to-reach "true" memories, if a person is asked the right sort of question (and it has the power to elicit "fake" memories, if a person is asked a misleading sort of question).

If no other answer had better support, then we could call this the Most Supported answer because it has some support, but it's hard to say just from this one study that hypnosis cannot do [ _____ ] .

**C. Recalling events under hypnosis inevitably results in false memories.** — Too Strong: inevitably (10% of students)
This falls into the same bucket as (C). We know that within this study, some false memories were created, but it's too strong to say from this one study that this always happens; it's inevitable; it cannot be otherwise.

**D. What people recall under hypnosis depends to at least some extent on suggestion.** — Correct (74% of students)
This is more softly-worded then other answers, so it's easier to support. Do we have any evidence that suggestion can play a role or have an effect on what people recall under hypnosis?

Sure! People recalled scenes from a films they never saw. Since they didn't see any film, the question posed by the researchers suggested to them that they had seen a movie. It may even be that the music piece they heard suggested certain emotions or feelings that the hypnotized people recalled as though they were scenes from a movie.

There is an annoying aspect of this answer to me that may have troubled some students --- is it improper to say that you're recalling something, if it's a false memory? It almost feels like this answer should have been written as "what people claim to recall under hypnosis".

But given how extreme the other contenders are and how soft and provable the wording here is, this is still our safest pick.

**E. Visual memory is enhanced more by hypnosis than is auditory memory.** — Unsupported Comparison (4% of students)
This study only featured hypnotized subjects trying to remember auditory memories. There were no visual memories they were being tested on recalling.

We couldn't possibly support such a sweeping broad comparison from this one study that hypnosis has more effect on visual memory than on auditory memory.

## S1 · Q7 — Main Conclusion

> Note: LSAT Lab offers only a single authored written explanation for this question (no Standard / Brief / Another Way selector).

### Stimulus
Ethicist: Robert Gillette has argued that because a thorough knowledge of genetics would enable us to cure the over 3,000 inherited disorders that affect humanity, deciphering the human genetic code will certainly benefit humanity despite its enormous cost. Gillette's argument is not persuasive, however, because he fails to consider that such knowledge might ultimately harm human beings more than it would benefit them.

### Prompt
7. Which one of the following most accurately expresses the conclusion of the ethicist's argument?

### Written (single style)
**Argument breakdown**
- **Conclusion (however):** Gillette's argument is not persuasive. (it's not clear that a thorough knowledge of genetics would certainly benefit humanity, just because it could cure diseases)
- **Evidence (because):** He fails to consider that such thorough knowledge of genetics might harm human beings more than it would benefit them.
- **Evaluate:** Whenever we're doing a Main Conclusion question, we prime our brains before reading the paragraph with the prevailing tendencies on Main Conclusion questions:
- Rather than put the Conclusion as the final claim, the conclusion on Main Conclusion questions is usually found earlier than the evidence. Typically, it's ... - the very first claim of the paragraph - it's rebutting some previous or external point of view that isn't the author's
- In this paragraph we start with an external point of view, Gillette's.
- Most of the time in LR and RC, after an author attributes a point of view to someone, she pivots away with a but / yet / however.
- That pivot doesn't necessarily mean we're hearing a conclusion.
- It could go: However, Conclusion. After all, Support. But it could also go: However, Support. Thus, Conclusion
- In this paragraph, the word because clearly indicates that the last idea is Support, so that makes "G's argument is not persuasive" our Conclusion.
- F.A.B.S. = for, after all, because, since (the four Support indicators you should memorize)
- **Goal:** Since this conclusion is using referential language, "Gillette's argument is not persuasive" we need to substitute-in the specific language being referred to. "The idea that deeply knowing genes will benefit humanity is not persuasive"
**Answer choices**
**A. Gillette's argument wrongly assumes that deciphering the genetic code will lead to cures for genetic disorders.** — Wrong Objection / Unstated (2% of students)
Our author is saying that Gillette's argument wrongly assumes that deciphering the genetic code, "will be a net benefit to humanity" / "will not lead to more harm than benefit"

**B. Deciphering the genetic code might ultimately harm human beings more than benefit them.** — Premise Last Claim Trap (27% of students)
This is the last claim of the paragraph, which was a premise. The last claim will sometimes be the correct answer on Main Conclusion but is inherently trappy.

In this case, this final claim is prefaced by a Support word, "because", so it's easy to be sure that this is not the Main Conclusion.

**C. Because of its possible negative consequences, genetic research should not be conducted.** — Too Strong: should not be conducted (1% of students)
The author's conclusion is, "Gillette has not convinced me that deciphering the genetic code will benefit humanity", not "we should do "no" genetic research".

**D. Gillette's claim that a thorough knowledge of genetics would enable us to cure over 3,000 disorders is overstated.** — Wrong Objection / Unstated (2% of students)
The author is saying, "I accept that we'll be able to cure more disorders, but deep knowledge of our genetic code might lead to harm that outweighs that benefit."

Her objection wasn't to Gillette's premise about curing disorders, it was to his conclusion that this will be a net benefit for humanity.

**E. Gillette's argument is unconvincing because it ignores certain possible consequences of genetic research.** — Correct (68% of students)
Yes, "Gillette's argument is unconvincing" is a perfect meaning match for "Gillette's argument is not persuasive".

This answer is an anomaly among correct answers to Main Conclusion questions, because it contains both the conclusion and a summary of the evidence.

Why would LSAC make this mistake? Not sure. But if our only objection to this answer is, "Hey, not fair, you have the Conclusion and the Premise", and our objection to every other answer is, "Hey, that's not the Conclusion", then this is our best available answer.
