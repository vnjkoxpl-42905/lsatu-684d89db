# Trap Master Decoy Catalog

**Synopsis**
Five trap patterns the LSAT uses on Role, Method, and Argument Part questions. Each ruins an otherwise-good answer with a single word or phrase. Reading for vibe gets you trapped. Reading for word-by-word survival breaks the trap.

**Trap 1: Synonym swap**
The answer uses a word close to what the stimulus says but not exact. Most becomes all. Some becomes many. Likely becomes must. Possible becomes will.
*Defense:* check every quantifier and every modal verb.

**Trap 2: Scope creep**
The answer describes a structural move that's slightly broader or narrower than what the stimulus does. Author *describes* an example; answer says author *advocates for* it.
*Defense:* check the verb against the verb taxonomy.

**Trap 3: Polarity flip**
The answer reverses what the author is doing. Author *attacks* a view; answer says author *defends* it.
*Defense:* identify the verb first. Then confirm the direction.

**Trap 4: Premise-conclusion swap**
The answer identifies all the right pieces but mislabels which is the conclusion and which is the support.
*Defense:* apply the directional-word check.

**Trap 5: Role mismatch**
The answer correctly describes a piece of the stimulus but assigns it the wrong slot. Calls evidence what is actually a concession. Calls a main conclusion what is actually intermediate.
*Defense:* apply the 7-slot check.

**Test for the right answer**
When two answers feel close, ask: *which one survives a word-by-word check?* The Trap Master answer falls apart on one specific word. The correct answer holds together on every word.
