# Translation Buckets

**Synopsis**
Every abstract LSAT phrase is doing one of five jobs. Identify the bucket, and you're already most of the way to the translation. Memorize the verb signatures for each.

**Assumption bucket**
The argument *requires* an unstated thing. Plain English: "depends on" or "requires."
Verb signatures: *presupposes, takes for granted, tacitly assumes, depends on, requires.*

**Causal bucket**
Cause-and-effect vocabulary. The "phenomenon" is the effect; the "hypothesis" or "explanation" is the proposed cause.
Verb signatures: *explains, accounts for, is responsible for, gives rise to, posits as the cause of, hypothesizes.*

**Comparison bucket**
The argument is mapping one case onto another. An analogy is a different specific case that resembles the case at hand in some general way.
Verb signatures: *is analogous to, parallels, resembles, mirrors, contrasts with.*

**Evidence / relevance bucket**
How strongly one piece of information backs another. "Most strongly supports" is the MBT / MSS stem giveaway.
Verb signatures: *lends support to, is consistent with, most strongly supports, is compatible with, corroborates.*

**Evaluation bucket**
The argument is being graded against a logical bar. "Undermines" and "calls into question" are soft-to-medium attacks. "Sufficient" and "necessary" are condition-strength words.
Verb signatures: *undermines, calls into question, casts doubt on, weakens, strengthens, refutes, is sufficient for, is necessary for.*

**How to use**
1. Read the abstract phrase.
2. Find the verb.
3. Match the verb to a bucket.
4. The bucket tells you what kind of plain-English claim the phrase maps to.

D2 is the drill that builds this reflex. D7 layers X/Y mapping on top.
