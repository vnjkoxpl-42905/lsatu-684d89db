# The Five-Bucket Decode Pass

**Synopsis**
When an LSAT answer choice or stimulus reads as opaque, scan it for the five translation-bucket verbs. The first bucket you hit usually decodes the whole phrase.

**The decode pass**
1. **Causal language?** Look for *phenomenon, hypothesis, explanation, accounts for, gives rise to.* If yes, the phrase is a cause-and-effect claim. Translate to "X caused Y" or "X is the proposed cause of Y."
2. **Assumption language?** Look for *presupposes, takes for granted, tacitly assumes.* If yes, the argument *requires* the unstated thing. Translate to "the argument needs X to be true."
3. **Comparison language?** Look for *analogous, parallels, contrasts with, resembles.* If yes, the argument is mapping one case onto another. Translate to "X is the case at hand; Y is the parallel case being used to illuminate it."
4. **Evidence language?** Look for *supports, lends support to, is consistent with, most strongly supports.* If yes, the phrase is grading how strongly one piece of information backs another. Translate to "X gives weight to Y" or "X doesn't conflict with Y."
5. **Evaluation language?** Look for *undermines, calls into question, casts doubt on, sufficient, necessary.* If yes, the argument is being graded against a logical bar. Translate to "X weakens Y" or "X strengthens Y" or "X is/isn't enough for Y."

**How to use**
Read the abstract phrase top to bottom. The moment you hit a verb that belongs in one of the five buckets, stop and translate that piece first. The rest of the phrase then folds in around the translation.

Most dense LSAT answers stack two or three buckets in one sentence. Decode them one bucket at a time.
