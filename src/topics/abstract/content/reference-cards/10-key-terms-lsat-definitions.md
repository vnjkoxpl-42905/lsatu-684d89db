# Key Terms (LSAT Definitions)

**Synopsis**
The seven key terms the LSAT uses in answer choices, with LSAT-specific definitions verbatim from the source keyword list.

| Term | LSAT definition |
|---|---|
| **Presuppose** | In daily usage, can mean assumption or necessary. On the LSAT, presuppose simply means "require." It refers to a necessary condition. |
| **Illustrate** | An illustration, as it appears in an LR question, is simply referring to an example or examples. |
| **General Principle** | A rule on what we should or shouldn't do. It applies to multiple conditions and scenarios. |
| **Facts** | Statements such as "Canada is the largest country in the world" or "Water consists of hydrogen and oxygen." Not arguments by themselves. Can serve as premises or background information. |
| **Decision** | A choice made, like "I will be taking the train from Canada to USA instead of Bus." Decisions can play roles as premises or conclusions. |
| **Principle** | A generalizable statement used to guide or justify (similar to a rule) future or past actions or decisions. Example: "Punishment should be proportional to the crime committed." Principles can be premises or conclusions. |
| **Generalization** | A summary statement derived from smaller findings, like "All swans are white." Generalizations describe what is, while principles prescribe what should or ought to be. They can function as premises or conclusions. |

**How to use**
When you see one of these words in an answer choice, mentally substitute the LSAT definition and check whether the stimulus actually exhibits that thing.
