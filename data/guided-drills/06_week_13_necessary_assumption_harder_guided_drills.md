# Week 13 — Necessary Assumption (Harder)

Two advanced-band Necessary Assumption drills. Both draw Necessary Assumption only:
no Strengthen, no Weaken, no Flaw, no Sufficient Assumption.

## 1. Necessary, Not Helpful

### Metadata

```yaml
title: Necessary, Not Helpful
category: Process
estimated_time: 1 hr
question_count: 8
difficulty_level: 4-5
question_type: Necessary Assumption
start_button_label: Start
```

### Pre-Start Instruction Card

#### Left Column

Eight Necessary Assumption questions at difficulty 4 and 5, mixed across both jobs an assumption
can do: supplying a missing idea, and ruling out an objection.

You do not get told which one you are looking at. The tell is in the argument. Conditional logic,
or a phrase repeated twice inside the paragraph, points at a missing idea. A recommendation, a
prediction, or a hypothesis offered to explain some curious background data points at an
objection.

At this difficulty both kinds are dressed to look like the other. A conditional stimulus can still
want a Defender, and a causal stimulus can still want a plain missing link.

#### Right Column

# Try these 8 questions, untimed

- Is there a **New Concept** in the conclusion that the evidence never defined? If so, what idea
  from the evidence is it being connected to?

- Is the author assuming two things are **similar**, or assuming they are **different**? Spell out
  the obvious version. At this level the obvious version is frequently the answer.

- If neither worked, argue the Anti-Conclusion and see what you had to assume.

- Run the negation test on your pick before you commit. It is the only check that separates
  necessary from merely helpful.

As you review these,

**correct answers -** which of the two jobs was it doing? Which question found it?

**incorrect answers -** **Too Strong**, a **Reversal** of the link you wanted, or an idea that
helps the argument without being required?

## 2. Block the Objection

### Metadata

```yaml
title: Block the Objection
category: Process
estimated_time: 1 hr
question_count: 8
difficulty_level: 4-5
question_type: Necessary Assumption
question_feature: Defender
start_button_label: Start
```

### Pre-Start Instruction Card

#### Left Column

Every Necessary Assumption does one of two jobs. It supplies an idea the author was clearly
thinking, or it rules out an objection that would hurt the argument.

These 8 questions are all the second kind. The **Defender** answer does not add a new link to
the argument. It stands in front of the argument and blocks something that would have knocked
it over.

That is why these answers so often feel like they come from outside the paragraph. The author
never mentioned the objection, so the correct answer names something that was never discussed
and tells you it is not happening. Test-takers throw those answers out for being "out of scope",
and that instinct is exactly what is being punished here.

These are difficulty 4 and 5. Expect the objection to be one you have to build yourself rather
than one the stimulus hands you.

#### Right Column

# Try these 8 questions, untimed

- Find the conclusion, then argue the **Anti-Conclusion**. Accept every word of the evidence and
  try to reach the opposite result anyway. What did you have to assume to get there?

- Whatever objection you just built, the author is assuming it is **not** the case. That is the
  answer, in some form.

- If the author is making a recommendation, predicting an outcome, or explaining a curious fact,
  the objection is usually an alternate explanation, a hidden downside, or a reason the plan
  fails on its own terms.

- Negate the answer you picked. If the argument still stands, it was not necessary.

As you review these,

**correct answers -** did it block an objection you had already thought of? If it blocked one you
had not, what question would have surfaced it?

**incorrect answers -** was it **Too Strong** for a necessary assumption? Did it defend against
something the argument never actually needed? Did it strengthen without being required?
